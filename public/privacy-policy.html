FocusShield Privacy policy

A straightforward explanation

Your browsing stays yours.

FocusShield blocks websites you choose. It does not send your blocklist or browsing activity to us.

In short: FocusShield keeps its settings in your browser. The extension does not operate an account or analytics service, and does not transmit your browsing data to the developer.

1. Information handled

FocusShield is designed to work without collecting personal information. To provide its blocking feature, it handles the following information on your device:

  • The domain names you choose to block, and whether each domain is paused.
  • Your on/off preference for the shield.
  • A temporarily saved domain while your browser asks you to grant permission to block it.
  • Web addresses needed by the browser to compare navigation requests against your blocking rules and, when a match is blocked, show the blocked page.

FocusShield does not read page contents, collect a browsing history, or ask you to provide an account, name, or email address.

2. How information is used

The information above is used only to save your preferences, apply the blocking rules you configure, and redirect matching pages to the FocusShield blocked page. URL matching and redirection are performed locally by the extension and your browser.

3. Storage and retention

Settings are stored using your browser's local extension storage. They are not synced by FocusShield to a developer-controlled server. Your settings remain in that storage until you change or remove them; browser behavior may determine what happens to extension data when you uninstall the extension.

4. Sharing and third parties

The developer does not receive, sell, rent, or share information handled by FocusShield. The extension contains no analytics or advertising service and makes no network requests to send your blocklist or browsing activity.

Your browser processes extension permissions, storage, and network requests under its own terms and privacy policy. FocusShield does not control the browser's data practices.

5. Permissions and your choices

FocusShield requests storage access to save settings, and optional access to websites so it can block only the domains you choose and authorize. The browser's declarative network request feature applies the corresponding rules. The extension also declares the active-tab permission; the current blocking flow does not use it to read page contents.

You can remove blocked domains, pause individual domains, turn off the shield, or revoke site access in your browser's extension settings. Revoking access can prevent FocusShield from blocking that site.

6. Policy changes

If the extension's data practices change, this page will be updated to explain the changes. The effective date at the top indicates when this policy was last revised.
